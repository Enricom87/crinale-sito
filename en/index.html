🎯 Why Crinale is different

Forecasts made for the ridge, not for all of Italy

National weather sites use the same formula from Bolzano to Palermo, and models that see the Apennines as a rounded hill. So they get wrong exactly what matters most here: the altitude at which it snows and how much snow stays.

  • 🧭
    The wind picks the model No blind averaging: with the Libeccio the 2 km models (ICON-2I and AROME), which see the clouds hitting the Tuscan side, take the lead; with the Grecale the best models for the Emilian side weigh more.
  • 🌡️
    The real snow level Not the theoretical freezing level: we use the wet-bulb temperature, lower the snow level when it snows hard and account for the cold air pooling in hollows such as Val di Luce and Lago Santo.
  • 📏
    Snow at every altitude At the base, halfway up and at the summit. Heavy and wet with the Libeccio, powdery in the cold, and less where the wind sweeps the ridges, from Gomito to Croce Arcana to Cimone.
  • 🔭
    Two engines, two horizons Local physics for the first 5 days; from day 6 to 30 about 300 scenarios plus the big-cold indices (NAO, AO, polar vortex).

We don't ask you to take our word for it: every day we compare our forecasts with the snow measured by the stations, and the results are public. 📊 Report card · Method and sources

The next few days

And the weekend? Saturday 28 November and Sunday 29 November: the best spot is Febbio.

The trend, from Thu 3 to Mon 7

From the weekend on, dry days with normal temperatures alternates with dry, mild days, with temperatures at altitude around zero. The news is the cold: in about half of the scenarios the air at 1500 m drops below −2 °C, especially around Saturday 5 December, exactly what snowmaking needs. Snow, on the other hand, is still a gamble: it shows up in about one scenario in ten.

🌍 The big picture: Atlantic, Arctic and stratosphere

The big picture is neutral: no decisive push.

NAO near zero in the second week: no clear push towards cold or towards high pressure (12 negative, 41 positive scenarios out of 100).

Polar vortex near normal (26 m/s): no particular signal from the stratosphere.

NAO and AO indices: NOAA Climate Prediction Center (GEFS ensemble). Polar vortex: wind at 10 hPa along 60°N, from GFS.
